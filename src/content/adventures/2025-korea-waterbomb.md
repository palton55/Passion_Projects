## The story

In early July, halfway through my study abroad semester in Tokyo, I flew from Narita to Seoul with friends for eight days. The trip was built around Waterbomb, the summer festival where the crowd and the performers spend the whole day soaking each other, but it turned into one of the fullest weeks of the whole semester.

### Day 1: Namsan, Jogyesa, and Gwanghwamun

We landed at Incheon in the afternoon and took the AREX airport railroad straight into Seoul Station. My base for the week was a capsule hotel in Euljiro, right in the middle of the old downtown, which turned out to be a great spot: a short walk or a couple of subway stops from almost everything we did.

We dropped our bags and went straight up Namsan to N Seoul Tower. The view from the top of the hill is the best way I know to understand how big Seoul is, with the city spreading out in every direction until it runs into the mountains. From there we walked to Jogyesa Temple, the head temple of Korean Buddhism. Its main hall and courtyard were hung with hundreds of lanterns, and standing under the lantern ceiling with office towers rising behind it made for a striking contrast.

We reached Gwanghwamun Gate just as the sun was setting behind it, with Bugaksan rising behind Gyeongbokgung Palace. Gwanghwamun Square had a Squid Game installation set up for the show's final season, complete with the giant doll from "Red Light, Green Light." We finished the night with a Mom's Touch chicken burger, a walk along the Cheonggyecheon stream, and the first of many banana milks from a convenience store.

### Day 2: The DMZ, then LoL Park

The next morning started early with a tour bus out of Hongdae, heading north toward the border. Our first stop was Imjingak, a park beside the Imjin River with the Freedom Bridge, the Bell of Peace, and the old rail line that used to run north. The border fence runs right past rice fields there, and it was strange to see farmland and razor wire side by side.

From there we went to Dora Observatory, where the UN and Korean flags fly over a viewing deck that looks straight into North Korea, and then into Tongilchon, a farming village inside the Civilian Control Line where people still live and grow crops. The last stop was Odusan Unification Observatory in Paju, where the Han and Imjin rivers meet. North Korea is only a few kilometers across the estuary, close enough to make out buildings on the other side. It was a heavy morning, and an important one.

The tour dropped us back in the city, and I headed straight to LoL Park in Jongno, Riot Games' League of Legends arena and the home of the LCK. As someone who played esports professionally, seeing the trophy cases, the champion figures, and the stage where so many of the best players in the world compete was a bit of a pilgrimage for me.

### Day 3: Gangnam, COEX, and Hongdae

Day 3 was for shopping and wandering. We started in Gangnam, made the obligatory Olive Young run for skincare, and then went to COEX, where the Starfield Library's wall of bookshelves is every bit as impressive in person as it is in photos. Lunch was a Korean set meal in the mall.

That night we went back to Hongdae, Seoul's university neighborhood, which on a Friday night is packed. There were street dancers performing at every corner and crowds filling every crossing. We had dinner, hit a club, and stayed out late.

### Day 4: Waterbomb

Waterbomb Seoul 2025 was held at KINTEX in Goyang, just northwest of the city. We took the subway out in the afternoon, armed with water guns, and were soaked within minutes. Water cannons fire from the main stage over the crowd all day, the performers spray the audience between songs, and everyone in the crowd is shooting at everyone else.

The lineup was a mix of K-pop and hip-hop acts, and one of my highlights was Lee Young Ji's set as the sun went down. Between the crowd, the music, and the water, it was an unforgettable day and the main event of the trip.

### Day 5: Gyeongbokgung in hanbok, and Yongsan

The day after Waterbomb we rented hanbok, the traditional Korean dress, and went to Gyeongbokgung Palace. Anyone wearing hanbok gets in for free, so the palace grounds were full of people dressed the same way. We walked past Gyeonghoeru Pavilion on its pond and up to Hyangwonjeong at the back of the grounds, and took far too many photos.

In the evening we went to Yongsan Electronics Market, a huge complex of shops selling everything from parts to consoles. Nearby I spotted a neon taco stand, which was funny enough to stop for a photo. We ended the night back in Euljiro, drinking Cass and Terra in the old printing alleys near the hotel and picking out ramyeon from a wall of options.

### Day 6: Lotte World Mall

We spent Day 6 at Lotte World Mall in Jamsil, at the base of Lotte World Tower, the tallest building in Korea. We had corn dogs and jjajangmyeon, browsed the Studio Ghibli shop, and took photos with a giant Pingu.

### Day 7: PC bang, Switch 2, and Myeongdong

No trip to Korea would be complete for me without a PC bang, the Korean gaming cafes that helped make the country the center of esports. We spent part of the afternoon at one near Dongdaemun playing League of Legends and Overwatch on their setups. Then we went to Myeongdong, where I bought a Nintendo Switch 2, and had one last jjajangmyeon dinner.

### Day 8: Heading home

On the last morning I took the AREX from Seoul Station back across to Yeongjong Island and Incheon Airport, grabbed a Paris Baguette sandwich, and flew back to Narita for the rest of the semester in Tokyo.
